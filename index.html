READ WITH CARE

How to understand a community map

A map can help people find shared information, but a pin is not proof and does not guarantee current conditions.

01

Check date and time

Look at when the observation happened. A report from earlier may no longer describe current conditions.

02

Read the description

Separate what a contributor says they directly saw from their interpretation. A vehicle or uniform alone may not identify an agency.

03

Understand the status

Read the map’s own legend and review labels. Do not assume a category means a claim was independently confirmed.

04

Protect privacy

Do not share names, faces, license plates, private addresses, immigration status or identifying details.

WHAT A MAP CAN’T TELL YOU

Context matters.
One pin is not a conclusion.

Community maps are made from reports shared by individuals. Reports can be delayed, incomplete, duplicated or mistaken. A map may not show every event, and an area without a pin does not prove nothing happened.

  • Do not treat a report as an official government record.
  • Do not assume a report is current just because it is visible.
  • Do not approach, follow, photograph or confront anyone to verify a report.
  • For urgent personal safety concerns, contact a trusted local organization or emergency service as appropriate.

COMMON QUESTIONS

People Over Papers map FAQ

Where is the People Over Papers map?

Browse reviewed community information on the map above. Reports are generalized and delayed to protect privacy.

Does this website collect or verify sightings?

Submissions are reviewed before publication. Approved information is generalized before publication; we do not publish identifying details or exact addresses.

Are reports proof that something is happening now?

No. Check the reported date, description and status. Community reports can be delayed or incomplete and should not be treated as official alerts.

Can I get immigration legal advice here?

No. This website is not a law firm and does not provide legal advice. Use the legal-service directories on our resources page to find a qualified provider.
